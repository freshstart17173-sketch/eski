now playing · week of 3 august

Three of the seven this week are worth your evening. blame! master edition is the one to start with — tsutomu nihei spends whole chapters on corridors and stairwells with nobody speaking, and samuel opoku gives Cibo a dryness the score ducks under on every line. One part, Dhomochevsky, is still unvoiced after three weeks, which is nine lines and about twenty minutes of somebody's time.

If you have read falling in love on the path to hell before, read it again with j. wren's rescore — no strings, just water and a single piano, and it turns the descent into something much colder. And barbaric: axe to grind leaves on Sunday, so if you want to hear four different people play a talking weapon, this is the week.

— mara, who runs this

blame! master edition
tsutomu nihei · 368 pages · 12 chapters

Nihei lets the architecture do the work — corridors that run for chapters, stairs that arrive nowhere, and almost nobody speaking. The score is low drones and structural hum, mapped so the pressure rises exactly as the descent does. samuel opoku's Cibo carries a dryness the music ducks under on every line, and imogen ash plays Killy tired rather than grim, which is the whole book in one choice.
