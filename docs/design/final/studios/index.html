three studios, three skins

The same three tools, built once and skinned three ways, so the comparison is about the styling and nothing else. Every version is live: drag a clip, record a line, drop a file, turn a page. Pick a column.

bed score effects voice — four kinds of sound, one legend, used in all nine
1press

Broadsheet as it stands, plus three things: role colour as a cap and a swatch, an icon on every control, and nothing else. Hairlines, square corners, near-white ground. The bet is that the studios only ever needed a legend.

2workbench

Light still, but hierarchy comes from surfaces rather than hairlines: a sunk canvas, raised panels, hover fills. Clips are filled in their role's colour, the ruler carries page thumbnails, controls grow to 30px with an icon and a word.

3console

Dark, dense, and unapologetically a tool — the ground every DAW and editor these people already use. 26px lanes, 12.5px type, a transport pinned at the foot, a playhead across every lane, clips filled solid in role colour.

what is the same in all nine

One data file, one behaviour file per studio. The nine pages differ by a stylesheet and three structural switches — thumbnails, playhead, transport foot — so anything you like in one column can be moved to another.

Sound effects are in the model now. The author writes them as their own entry type, with the lettering as drawn (KRAK) and a description. Every unfilled one shows up in the composer's cue rail as a page and a description waiting for a file, and in the performer's script so they can hear what is coming.

The page crop is fixed. The image had a percentage height resolving against a parent sized by the image; it now sits in a frame with a definite height and letterboxes properly.

Not built yet: profile (which absorbs the shelf, broadsheet with a little colour and personality) and the reader.
