the ledger

.claude/skills/eski-ledger open the mockup →

A comic is a release, a mix is a pressing, and every person who touched it gets a row.

THE REFERENCE

Discogs release pages, plus Genius credits and the liner-note tradition behind both.

Discogs is the only mainstream product that treats "one work, many versions, dozens of humans who each did one job" as the primary UI problem. No cards, no hero, no marketing copy — a masthead and a credits block where every line reads role, then person.

WHY IT FITS

The current detail page shows a cast as off-by-default checkboxes, which frames credit as a settings panel. It isn't one. Credit is the whole economy — contributors show up because their name lands somewhere permanent.

The version bar replaces the mix picker: you are always looking at one mix, and switching is one click.

WHAT IT MOVES

Roadmap 5 — the comic page with real urls; this is what lives at /c/<slug>.

Roadmap 6 — an uncast character is just an empty row with a "voice this" action. The contribute hub becomes a query rendered inline, not a second page.

Roadmap 12 — kudos and vouches already have a right-aligned number column.

owns: comic detail, cast bar, contribute hub, profiles

the marquee

.claude/skills/eski-marquee open the mockup →

With twenty comics, a grid advertises an empty library and a marquee advertises taste.

THE REFERENCE

MUBI, and behind it the Criterion Channel.

MUBI's founding constraint was thirty films, one added and one removed daily. That forced a UI most streaming design refuses: no rows of rows, one title per screen, and a paragraph of real writing under it. The scarcity is the pitch.

WHY IT FITS

Cold start is the actual product problem. A grid of twelve covers makes the platform look abandoned; "seven comics this week, three still need voices" makes the same catalogue look programmed.

It commits to dark in both themes so the cover art is the only saturated thing on screen — which is what tokens.css already argues for.

WHAT IT MOVES

Roadmap 5 — a marquee entry is the open-graph card: cover, title, one sentence, cast status.

Roadmap 6 — "needs one voice, dhomochevsky, 9 lines" sits in the rotation caption, so recruiting happens on the home page rather than a page nobody visits.

owns: signed-out home, browse, collections, share previews

the rack

.claude/skills/eski-rack open the mockup →

You cannot judge a voice actor from their name, so every name on the site should play.

THE REFERENCE

Bandcamp, with NTS Radio for the chrome.

Bandcamp's structural decision is that the player is part of the page, not a destination. Play affordances live on list rows, tags are the taxonomy, and the audio never stops when you navigate. The plainness is why the music reads as the point.

WHY IT FITS

Checkboxes ask you to choose between two names you have never heard. A rack lets you hear both in eight seconds without leaving the page.

Open roles and cast members become the same component — a row with a play triangle — so the contribute hub costs almost no new UI.

WHAT IT MOVES

Roadmap 8 — preview clips are the whole thesis, not a nice-to-have.

Roadmap 6 — the contribute hub is one list of the same row.

Roadmap 7 — swapping mixes mid-read is the same rack, docked in the reader.

owns: browse, contribute hub, profiles, previews everywhere

the session

.claude/skills/eski-session open the mockup →

A comic scored page by page is a matrix, not a timeline, and musicians have read that matrix since 2001.

THE REFERENCE

Ableton Live's Session View, with Splice for browse-and-audition.

Live has two views of one song: a timeline, and a grid where columns are tracks, rows are scenes, and firing a scene launches everything in that row. When a piece is built from discrete triggered chunks, the grid beats the timeline.

WHY IT FITS

The roadmap already says the soundtrack timeline goes away and each page's panel becomes where you author that page. That is a description of Session View — and an eski page turn is a scene launch.

Status colour stays exactly where tokens.css put it: two shades of one hue for the soundtrack states, a different hue for one-shots.

WHAT IT MOVES

Roadmap 10 — the whole composer streamline, in one shape rather than eleven separate fixes.

Roadmap 11 — pre-export validation stops being an error message. Two starts in one column, tracks past the last page and empty rows are all visible on the grid.

owns: composer studio, page grid, media bay, export checks

the script

.claude/skills/eski-script open the mockup →

The dialogue is already written down, so the script is the timeline and nobody should have to scrub a waveform to find line 14.

THE REFERENCE

Descript, with screenplay format for the page furniture.

Descript's move is that the transcript is the edit. It works because speech already has a canonical textual form, so the text view loses almost nothing and gains random access, search and readability. Screenplay format contributes character cues and parentheticals.

WHY IT FITS

A contributor's job is: read these lines, as this character, in order. Handing them a DAW is a category error and the most likely reason a casual contributor bounces.

It also makes author review tractable. Reading a script with a play triangle per line is a two-minute job; auditing a forty-minute continuous track is not.

WHAT IT MOVES

Roadmap 1 — consent is legible here: a performer sees whose comic and which role before recording a word.

Roadmap 3 — per-line takes give moderation something specific to act on instead of a whole track.

Roadmap 9 — take stacks are the natural unit for autosave and resumable upload.

owns: voiceover studio, recording flow, author review of submitted parts

against the current design docs

In the .dc.html mockupsWhat these directions do insteadWhy
Pink accent marking "the human layer" Dropped in all five A second brand hue buys one signal and costs the rule that made the system coherent — one hue, art is the only colour. The human layer is better marked by an avatar, a name and a play triangle, all of which are already there.
Mix as a checkbox list A version bar (ledger) or an auditionable row (rack) Checkboxes ask you to choose between names you have never heard, and imply a mix is a setting rather than a credited performance.
Comic detail as a right-hand drawer A page with an address A drawer cannot be linked, previewed, or arrived at, which is exactly what roadmap item 5 exists to fix.
One studio split into three role-locked mockups Two shapes: a grid for scoring, a document for voicing Roles differ by the kind of work, not by permissions. Composing is placement; voicing is reading aloud. Those want genuinely different surfaces.
VOICED / SCORED badges on covers Words in a caption: "needs one voice, dhomochevsky, 9 lines" A badge says a state. A sentence says a state and asks for something.

HOW TO USE THESE

Each direction is a skill in .claude/skills/, so you can say "build the contribute hub with eski-rack" and get the whole rule set applied rather than a screenshot copied. The skills are additive to eski-brand — they inherit tokens.css and override only what the style needs.

They are not mutually exclusive. The ledger and the rack are the same information at two densities and would ship together. The session and the script cover different halves of the studio and both are needed. The marquee is the only one that competes with anything — it replaces the cover grid on the home page and nothing else.

If you want one to start with: the ledger. It is the comic page from roadmap item 5, it is the highest-traffic surface you do not yet have, and it absorbs the contribute hub for free.
